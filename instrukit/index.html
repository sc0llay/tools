Differential pressure at max flow
—
Flow against DP
Mass flow qm = C / √(1−β⁴) · ε · π/4 · d² · √(2 ΔP ρ₁), solved iteratively because C depends on the Reynolds number and ε on the DP. Orifice C is Reader-Harris / Gallagher (ISO 5167-2), classical Venturi uses the fixed ISO 5167-4 values (machined 0.995, cast 0.984, rough-welded 0.985), the ISA 1932 nozzle uses ISO 5167-3. Gas expansibility ε follows the same standards. DP is the differential across the taps; the mA (DP) column is a linear 4–20 mA on DP with 4 mA at zero and 20 mA at max flow; with square-root extraction the output is linear in flow instead. Limits shown are abridged ISO 5167 ranges: check the standard and the manufacturer's calculation for custody or safety duty. Density and viscosity must be at flowing conditions; for gas, use absolute upstream pressure.

Thermocouple types
Thermocouple cable colours
Identify an unknown cable by its negative leg: IEC 60584-3 is always white, BS 1843 always blue, ANSI always red. DIN 43710 is the odd one out — there the positive leg is always red.

BS 1843:1952 is withdrawn but still all over older UK plant; new UK and EU installations follow BS EN 60584-3, which is identical to IEC 60584-3. Under IEC 60584-3 a light blue sheath marks cable for an intrinsically safe circuit.

Colours above are for extension cable (type X — same alloys as the thermocouple). Compensating cable (type C, e.g. KCA / KCB, RCA / SCA) uses substitute alloys and carries the same sheath colour, so check the printed legend rather than the colour alone before you trust it on a noble-metal loop.
